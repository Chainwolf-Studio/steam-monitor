Please note: this update includes a version increase, so any run in progress will be wiped.
If you're partway through a run you care about, finish it before updating. Your unlocks and meta-progression are not affected.
Fixes and Changes

Save fix for former demo players:
if you played the demo before buying the full game, your progress could fail to save. That's fixed.

Shred and Ignite are simpler:
any hit that deals hull damage now applies its full Shred or Ignite value. Before, the amount applied was capped by how much hull damage got through, so a hit that mostly landed on shields applied less than the card said. This works both ways: it applies to enemy cards too.

Route preview:
hover over any node on the star map to see the route there. It automatically picks the route that uses the least fuel.

Mission highlights:
hovering over mission text that mentions a specific node now highlights that node on the map.

Better mission placement:
missions no longer spawn on nodes you can't reach, or halfway across the map. They now only pick nodes you can actually reach within the mission's jump distance.
The quit button now has a tooltip explaining where your progress is automatically saved.
Pulse Laser Changes
The Pulse Laser gets two new cards and some tuning to make its library more flexible alongside other weapons.
New cards:

Counter Pulse
- 2O 1E, CD 5 - Deal 3 damage. If the targeted equipment is about to fire: deal +3 damage.

Steady Pulse
- 2O 1E, CD 6 - Deal 5 damage. Costs no Ordnance if you played a Tactical card this turn.
Changes:
Quick Pulse (+): cost reduced from 2O 1E to 2O. Quick Pulse now deals 4 damage (was 5); Quick Pulse+ still deals 6. Cooldown 3.
Snap Fire (+): the cooldown reduction now applies to any attack in your cooldown pile, not just Laser attacks.
Test Fire (+): now discounts your next 2 attacks this turn by 2 Ordnance each (3 Ordnance each for Test Fire+), instead of a single attack.
That's it for this week, see you in the next one!

This was a really productive month, and the game is closer to a playable build than ever. It’s really starting to come together, so much so that I’ll probably need a new trailer in a month or two.
New additions
Bug nests
Enemy bug bases spawn at random spots around your base and start producing bugs, which sit idle until it’s time to attack.
For now, bug bases have 3 buildings: nests (bug spawners), walls and bug turrets.
Between waves they grow, one nest at a time. Leave them alone for too long and you’ll be facing a wave that’s too big to handle.
Idle bugs guard their base, while worker bugs repair damaged buildings and construct new ones.
Base upgrades
I wanted a way to upgrade that works differently from research. Some things are capped: the number of troops you can control, and the territory you can build on. To raise those limits, you do a base upgrade: you start the upgrade, place upgrade pods and deliver the required items to them. Once enough materials have been delivered, the upgrade is done. A few examples:i
You start with a maximum of 100 troops. To raise that to 200, you research Basic Troop Control, start a base upgrade, place the upgrade pods, and deliver 15 steel frames and 20 electric circuits to a pod, which takes 10 seconds to process them. Do that 100 times and you can control 200 troops instead of 100. Of course, it’s meant to be automated.
Base upgrades need the same materials you use to recruit troops and make research packs, so once an upgrade is done, the production lines you built for it can be repurposed.
The minimap and the wave timer and direction indicator are also unlocked through base upgrades. It’s as if you’re upgrading your base building itself.
Better pathfinding
The old pathfinding was bad at times, and troops often got stuck on building corners. I’m still improving it, but no units have gotten stuck with the new version so far.
Better particles and atmosphere
I updated the particles a lot this month. Every shot now leaves a tracer (I really like how it looks), ants kick up dust as they walk, and dust particles float around all the time. And look at the bug nest particles! I think they look great, and a bit spooky.
Better UI
I’ve updated the UI to make it look more professional. Besides making the buildings and windows look nicer, I added troop range: when you select troops, you can see where they’re able to shoot.
What’s next
This month I’ll keep focusing more on the RTS side.
More troops
Right now there are only 2 ant troops: the Harasser, fast moving and quick to reload but with low HP and damage, and the Sharpshooter, a slow, medium-HP sniper. Coming up:
Heavy ant:
deals low-to-medium melee damage to all enemies around it. Its job is to draw the enemy’s attention and soak up damage while your ranged troops do the killing.
Assault tank:
the first vehicle. Fast but low damage, more of a skirmisher. Good for opening a front, bad at staying in a long fight.
Light mobile artillery:
great for sieges, but slow and fragile. If enemies reach it, it’s done for.
Support unit:
a healer that can also repair your buildings.
Transport vehicle:
troops are slow, and logistics matter in war. The transport carries 10–15 troops at once and starts the troop logistics mechanic, which can be automated: build stations, send troops to one to board, and the transport carries them to another station and drops them off. Trains will come later and work in a similar way. This should make long wars of attrition sustainable without you micromanaging everything.
New bug troops
A heavy bug with high damage.
A ranged bug.
Bugs will also get a smaller nest building that spawns bugs too, plus another turret. I’m still working out the details.
New mechanics planned before the playtest
Energy for troops:
right now there’s no downside to keeping 1,000 idle troops. Troops will draw from a shared energy buffer, powered by special buildings connected to the electric grid. Energy use will ramp up fast as your army grows, so you’ll need to keep up with production.
The Hive:
in the game’s lore, you’re one expedition sent out by a hive-mind civilization. The Hive will now give you missions and rules. Complete missions, like sending troops back to the Hive by rocket or destroying a particular enemy base, and you’ll be rewarded with bonuses. ## 29. Spirit of Europe - Enlightenment

- **AppID:** 4642020
- **Title:** Devlog 12 - Are we there yet?
- **Published:** 2026-10-05T08:00:21Z
- **Author:** (not provided)
- **URL:** https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/4642020/view/670623586021542492

### Announcement text

With the world built and the game finally playable from start to finish, we reached that magical stage of development where everything is technically there and working, yet the game is unplayable and most definitely not fun!
Some were the usual technical suspects: save/load issues, menu oddities, settings not behaving, transitions acting strange, tutorials not always showing when they should, some audio bugs, buttons misbehaving, missing clears, missing checks, missing portraits, missing warnings - the standard “how is this even connected to that?” kind of nonsense.
Then came the real game-maker part for this genre: balance.
There have been endless iterations on the data to make it both feel good and stay grounded historically. And because of that, there are some built-in imbalances that need working around. In other games you can just mix and match until things fit. In this game, some things were simply the way they were, and there was no getting around it: Paris was the centre of the Enlightenment and all its personality slots are filled to the brim, while other locations barely have anyone. Since these things needed to stay accurate, balancing was not the usual “throw a maths model at it and stay within some acceptable deviation” sort of job. Things had to be tailored and actually tested through playthroughs.
Pacing was also something that needed addressing since a turn needed to have enough action going on, otherwise players might just spam turns while staring at the resource indicators.
Of course, some things couldn’t just be magically fixed with numbers, but we had to add some additional features to make sure the game feels the way we intend. Among those, we added objectives at certain milestone turns.
After endless iterations across a dozen data tables, we finally hit a point where things are close enough to what we’ve envisioned. Naturally, all developers eventually need to accept that no game is ever truly “finished”, but merely released at a point where it has become stable enough to be truly scrutinized by actual players.
So yes, this last stretch was messy, repetitive and occasionally frustrating, yet completely necessary, which means the development ended exactly the way game development usually ends.
(July 2026)
