## 17. Spirit of Europe - Enlightenment

- **AppID:** 4642020
- **Title:** Devlog 11 - Where 3D?
- **Published:** 2026-09-28T06:00:12Z
- **Author:** (not provided)
- **URL:** https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/4642020/view/670623586021542490
- **GID:** 670623586021542490

### Announcement text

At this point, most game systems were in place and largely working, the UI was there, content was there, we still needed some portraits and whatnot, but otherwise it was “all done”.
So this phase was about building the actual 3D world.
That sounds simple enough, until you actually try to figure out what kind of 3D world this game should even have. Because this is not a city builder, not a character action game, not a grand strategy map in the usual sense but something… different. It needed to feel atmospheric, historical, not too stylized, and light enough to run on older machines, since we figured peeps rolling enthusiast-grade hardware wouldn’t be into our little game.
So, we spent quite a while trying to figure out the art direction since we already had the UI to our liking and the 3D needed to also fit well with it.
The big issue was always balance. If the map is too bare, the game feels dead. If it is too detailed, laptops heat up fast and FPS drops. If it leans too hard into realism, we’re back to the tech problem, aside from countless hours of content making which we simply don’t have. Then again, if it leans too hard into abstraction, then why bother making it 3D in the first place. So we had to settle on symbolism - just like the whole ethos of our little trip through the spirit world where Ambition, Fear and Reason come to visit us as spirits of the ethos of ages. Whether or not it’s the best idea, we’ll never know. But given the constraints, it’s the best we’ve had!
This stage included a lot of work on the map itself, node visuals, roads, foliage, materials, VFX and the general visual composition of the world. Obviously, all of this took quite a while since we also had to figure out scale, density, contrast, colour, and how much visual information the map can carry in late-game without having laptop owners cancel us.
We are aware of the fact that on paper, this basically summarizes to “so, we built the world”. But in truth, there are up to 6 development slots per city and two dozen cities with their own identity and architectural style. While we did settle on symbolic representations of these cities through an arch and obelisk loosely fitting their respective architectural flavor, making all of that was a grind - a fun and satisfying one, but a grind nonetheless.
This stage finally had us look upon “the game”. Here it was, ready to be played.
So we started, and… A bug here, one there, some events are too taxing, others too often, some factions fight too much, some research is too expensive, some developments don’t justify their costs, the starting resources are too little, there’s no incentive for the player to expand… Need I say more? It was a mess.
So, we did what all developers do at this stage: pat each other on the back saying what a good job we did, told everyone “it’s built, packaged and ready to publish”; then silently called in some QA peeps.
(June 2026)
